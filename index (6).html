📝 Phase 2 · Reflection

Educational Setting & Learner Profile

Students aged 9–12 at Starskids show consistent improvement in English language skills. They are familiar with common vocabulary, understand basic grammar, respond well to instructions given in the classroom, and are active participants in play-based activities at the A1–A2 level. Their strengths show a positive attitude towards learning and a foundation for developing more effective responses in the form of sentences.

The chosen educational setting is an English private school with students aged between 5 and 8 years old at the A1 level. At this level, students are able to identify basic vocabulary and relate it with the use of images, understand basic sentences, and follow instructions. Students are mostly fond of playing and listening to music and are drawn to colorful materials. One of the major challenges the students face is speaking — they mostly repeat vocabulary rather than forming full sentences. However, they are good at listening and responding well when exposed to familiar vocabulary. The room is equipped with a computer, internet connection, a projector, and sometimes tablets.

Novel Methodology: Task-Based Intercultural Learning + Flipped Classroom

To address these needs, Task-Based Intercultural Learning (TBIL) was combined with the Flipped Classroom method. TBIL is based on the use of meaningful tasks that link language with real-life and cultural contexts (Cai, 2024; Wang, 2004). Students learn vocabulary and simple phrases at home using videos or simple games, then participate in speaking tasks in class. This gives the class more time for student interaction and practice, which is essential for young learners. As suggested by Francl (2014), flipped learning is effective when used for active learning and engagement in face-to-face sessions.

Technology Tools: Wordwall & Quizlet

For technology use, Wordwall and Quizlet were identified as effective tools for learning. Wordwall was used to develop interactive games for learning and practicing vocabulary and listening through matching games or speaking tasks. Quizlet was used for learning and practicing vocabulary through flashcards and games that support reading and listening. These tools were effective for young learners since they include visuals that help them learn and remember. According to König et al. (2022), the use of ICT in class can increase students' engagement and cater to students with different learning styles. The tools therefore help students progress from identifying language to using it with confidence in speaking activities.

📚 Phase 3 · Lesson Plan 1

Talking About Home and Family

Flipped Classroom + Task-Based Intercultural Learning

A1 Level Ages 5–8 Speaking & Listening
Learning Objective
Students will identify and pronounce basic vocabulary related to home life (family members, rooms, objects) and ask/answer simple questions using "Where is…?" and "Who is…?"
Learning Outcome
Students present a simple map of their house and introduce family members and rooms using basic English vocabulary during a role-play activity.
Approach & Methodology
Flipped Classroom + Task-Based Intercultural Learning (TBIL). Pre-class video watch, in-class pair role-play (Visitor/Host), listening quiz, and mini-whiteboard activities.
Target Vocabulary
mom, dad, brother, sister, kitchen, bedroom, table, chair, bed — using "This is the kitchen." / "My mom is in the kitchen."
Key Technology
Google Classroom (LMS), YouTube pre-class video, Flashcards, Home Map Worksheet, Audio recordings, Mini-whiteboards
Assessment
Role-play speaking test (40%) · Listening quiz (30%) · Mini-whiteboard activity (20%) · Reflection participation (10%)
Pre-Task · Flipped Classroom

Pre-Class (Home)

Students watch a short video about rooms in a house and family members, repeat vocabulary words, and draw a simple picture of their house and family members to bring to class.

During Task (In Class)

Teacher introduces objective and vocabulary using flashcards. Students review through a circle game. Problem scenario introduced: "Your friend from another country is visiting your house. Show them your home in English." Students work in pairs (Visitor / Host) using a Home Map Worksheet.

Report

Each pair shares their home map role-play with the class, explaining rooms, objects, and who is in each room.

Post-Task

Listening quiz: students hear sentences and choose the correct picture. Teacher provides feedback on pronunciation, vocabulary use, and sentence formation.

📚 Lesson Plan 2 · Colors

Colors — Gamified Learning

Flipped Classroom + Task-Based Intercultural Learning · 120 minutes

Pre-A1 Starters Kids April 18, 2026
SMART Objective
By the end of 120 minutes, students will identify and orally name at least 4 basic colors (red, blue, yellow, green, pink, white, black, orange, purple) through interactive digital and physical activities with 80% accuracy.
Digital Tools
Google Classroom · Wordwall (memory game) · Kahoot (gamified assessment) · Sheppard Software (drag-and-drop) · Plays.org (color game)
Physical Materials
Hula hoops (color sorting circles) · Flashcards · Plastic animals & colored blocks/balls
Assessment
Kahoot Quiz: minimum 5/9 correct (80% target) · "I See Colors!" Production Task: students correctly name at least 5 colors independently · 3-point teacher checklist per student
📚 Lesson Plan 3 · Colors & Classroom Objects

Colors & Classroom Objects — AI-Supported

Multisensory Learning (See–Hear–Say) + Task-Based Learning + AI Interaction · 60 minutes

Pre-A1 May 12, 2026 ChatGPT
SMART Objective
Students identify and describe classroom objects and colors using "It's a/an + object. It's + color." through See–Hear–Say activities and AI-supported interaction with at least 80% accuracy.
Technology Integration
See–It–Hear–It–Say–It website (visual/auditory input) · ChatGPT (AI-supported speaking practice: "What color is your book?") · Teams (virtual classroom platform)
Flipped Component
Before class: short introductory video on colors and classroom objects, link to See–It–Hear–It–Say–It, and vocabulary list shared through Teams.
Target Vocabulary
Colors: red, blue, green, yellow, black · Objects: book, chair, table, pencil · Structures: "It's [color]." / "What color is it?" / "I see [color]!"
Assessment
Formative: teacher observation during Activities 4, 5, 6; ChatGPT interaction monitoring. Summative: final oral production — students describe at least 2–3 classroom objects with correct colors independently (80% accuracy target).
Problem Anticipation
Students may struggle to recall vocabulary or mix up word order. Differences in participation levels expected. Distractions or unfamiliarity with digital tools could reduce engagement.
📝 Phase 4 · Reflective Journal

Personal Learning Experience

Through this teaching experience, I acquired valuable knowledge about the practical use of Artificial Intelligence in language education, particularly in EFL contexts with young learners. One of the most important skills I developed was learning how to integrate AI — not as a replacement for the teacher, but as a tool that supports interaction and speaking practice. Using ChatGPT during the lesson showed how AI can scaffold communication by asking simple, level-appropriate questions and encouraging students to respond confidently.

My understanding of AI's role in teaching evolved significantly throughout this task. Initially, I saw AI mainly as a technological resource for content generation. However, after implementing the lesson, I realized that AI can work as an interactive learning tool that increases student engagement and participation. Students were motivated, ready to respond, and less anxious when speaking, which demonstrated that AI can create a supportive environment for language use. When AI is aligned with clear objectives and pedagogy, it becomes a powerful tool to enhance communicative competence rather than just a novelty in the classroom.

AI Tool Exploration and Evaluation

During the process of selecting and evaluating the AI tool, different applications were compared based on their suitability for speaking practice, usability, and alignment with learners' needs. ChatGPT was chosen because it allowed flexible, contextualized interaction and could be easily adapted to the learning objectives. Its main strengths are its intuitive interface, immediate responses, and ability to simulate simple conversations that encourage student participation.

In practice, ChatGPT proved effective in supporting language learning by reducing students' anxiety and increasing their willingness to speak. Its role as an interactive partner helped students use vocabulary meaningfully rather than mechanically. Clear teacher guidance proved essential to ensure appropriate language level and focus. Overall, the tool had a positive impact on engagement, fluency, and confidence in the EFL classroom.

Collaborative Work

The group collaborated closely in designing the rubric, activities, and overall class structure by sharing responsibilities and making joint decisions. Learning objectives were discussed together with a focus on speaking skills through colors and classroom objects. Each member contributed ideas for integrating the See–It–Hear–It–Say–It website and evaluating different AI tools, which helped in designing a clear checklist and selecting ChatGPT as the most suitable option. The rubric was created collaboratively to ensure it assessed pronunciation, participation, and correct use of target expressions.

One challenge faced was aligning everyone's ideas into a coherent lesson plan and managing time effectively. However, this was also a success, as open communication and flexibility allowed the group to reach consensus. Overall, teamwork strengthened the quality of the lesson and made the implementation smoother and more effective.

Lesson Design and Delivery

Preparing and teaching the class was a positive and meaningful experience, requiring careful planning to align objectives, activities, and technology. Designing the lesson around colors and classroom objects using the See–Hear–Say sequence ensured a clear progression from input to production. During the class, students were highly engaged, confirming that the activities were age-appropriate and well scaffolded.

The integration of ChatGPT and the LabINNOVA resource (See–It–Hear–It–Say–It) was highly effective. The website provided strong visual and auditory support for vocabulary acquisition, while ChatGPT enhanced speaking practice by encouraging students to respond to simple questions in a communicative way. This combination reduced anxiety and increased participation, especially during speaking tasks.

What worked particularly well was the students' enthusiasm and willingness to interact, as well as the smooth transition between activities. If the lesson were taught again, improvements would include allowing more time for individual AI interaction and preparing backup activities in case of technical issues. Overall, the lesson demonstrated how thoughtful integration of ICT and AI can enhance EFL learning.

✍️ Phase 5 · Reflective Essay

The Impact of Technology on EFL Teaching with Young Learners

Carolina Acevedo Mouawd & Richard Riaño Peña · Course 518006

ChatGPT CLT Approach Pre-A1
Introduction

The integration of technological tools in English as a Foreign Language (EFL) instruction has become increasingly relevant in promoting meaningful and engaging learning experiences. This reflective essay is based on a teaching experience with Pre-A1 young learners, in which a lesson on colors and classroom objects was implemented using a variety of digital and interactive resources. The educational setting involved the use of tools such as the See–It–Hear–It–Say–It website, Google Classroom, and the artificial intelligence tool ChatGPT, all integrated within a communicative and multisensory approach. These technologies were selected to support listening, speaking, and interaction through visual, auditory, and participatory activities adapted to young learners' needs. It is argued that, when used with clear pedagogical objectives, technology significantly enhances language learning by fostering interaction, motivation, and communicative competence among young learners.

Paragraph 1 · Describing the Experience

During the implementation of the lesson, technology played a central role in structuring and delivering instruction. The class was designed for Pre-A1 young learners and focused on teaching colors and classroom objects through a multisensory and communicative approach. The See–It–Hear–It–Say–It website was used to introduce vocabulary through visual and auditory input, allowing students to observe images, listen to pronunciation, and repeat words following a structured see–hear–say sequence. Additionally, ChatGPT was incorporated as an AI tool to facilitate speaking practice by asking simple, contextualized questions such as "What color is your book?" or "What is blue in your classroom?", encouraging students to respond orally using target expressions. One of the most successful experiences with technology was the AI-supported speaking activity, where students actively participated and showed enthusiasm when interacting with the tool. The combination of digital resources and interactive tasks allowed for a dynamic lesson in which students were not only exposed to language input but were also able to practice and produce language in a meaningful and engaging way.

Paragraph 2 · Analyzing the Impact

The integration of technological tools had a significant impact on both student learning and teaching practice, particularly in terms of engagement, interaction, and communicative development. Students showed increased motivation and participation, especially during the AI-supported activity, where they felt encouraged to respond orally in a low-pressure environment. This aligns with research on AI in EFL contexts, which highlights how interactive tools can promote autonomy, immediate feedback, and active language use, enhancing learners' confidence and willingness to speak. From a pedagogical perspective, technology also supported the implementation of a Communicative Language Teaching (CLT) approach, as students were not only exposed to vocabulary but were actively using language in meaningful contexts through guided questions and real-time interaction. However, some challenges emerged: ensuring all students understood how to interact with digital tools required additional scaffolding, and there is a potential risk of overreliance on AI, which may limit critical thinking or reduce teacher–student interaction if not carefully managed. Technical issues, such as connectivity or limited access to devices, can also affect the flow of the lesson.

Paragraph 3 · Personal Reflection & Conclusion

This experience allowed a deeper understanding of how technology can be meaningfully integrated into language teaching, especially with young learners. Tools such as ChatGPT and the See–It–Hear–It–Say–It resource are not simply complementary materials, but powerful elements that can transform the learning environment when aligned with clear pedagogical objectives. The perspective on AI in education changed significantly — it is now seen as an interactive partner that supports communication and reduces students' anxiety, rather than just a tool for content generation. Additionally, the importance of teacher guidance in ensuring that technology remains purposeful and accessible for all learners became clear. If the lesson were implemented again, more structured guidance during digital tool use and additional time for individual AI interaction would be provided, along with alternative activities prepared for technical difficulties. Overall, this experience reinforced that effective technology integration requires careful planning, flexibility, and reflection, strengthening the commitment to using innovative tools to enhance communicative competence in EFL contexts.

# Item Name Description
1 Name of Resource See It, Hear It, Say It! – An Interactive English Learning Resource for Young Learners
2 Type of Resource Digital Educational Resource — designed to support young children's early English language development (ages 2–5) through three foundational skill areas: Listening (recognizing familiar words, sounds, and simple instructions through songs, audio prompts, and repetition), Speaking (repeating words, naming objects, and responding with single words or short phrases), and Pre-writing / Motor Skills (tracing lines, shapes, and letters).
3 Problem to be Solved Children between 2 and 5 years old who are beginning to learn English need engaging and developmentally appropriate experiences. At this stage, they are still developing attention span, motor skills, and basic language awareness. Traditional methods often focus too early on reading and writing, which can be frustrating. There is a need for playful, interactive resources that support early English exposure through listening, speaking, and exploration, making learning natural and enjoyable.
4 Purpose To introduce English in a fun and engaging way, supporting early language development through listening, repetition, and play-based interaction.
5 Learning Objectives By completing this resource, children (ages 2–5) will be able to: (1) Listening: Recognize and respond to at least 5–8 familiar English words (colors, animals, objects). (2) Speaking: Repeat or say simple words and short expressions with teacher support. (3) Pre-writing: Trace basic lines, shapes, or letters and interact with visual elements on screen.
6 Academic Content Early English exposure through familiar and meaningful topics: Colors · Animals · Everyday objects · Numbers. Content presented through songs, repetition, visuals, and simple interaction — not formal reading or writing tasks.
7 Multimedia Resources Audios: Short, clear pronunciation clips, sound effects, and songs to promote listening and repetition. Videos: Brief animated videos (15–30 sec) presenting vocabulary through movement, music, and repetition. Images: Bright, colorful, age-appropriate illustrations of animals, objects, and colors. Interactive Slides: Click, tap, and drag activities for matching images and exploring content. Songs and Chants: Repetitive, rhythmic songs for participation, memory, and pronunciation. Tracing Animations: Visual guides for tracing lines, shapes, or basic letters.
8 Learning Strategy Play-based and gamified learning approach appropriate for early childhood education. Activities are short, visual, and interactive, promoting: attention and engagement · repetition and imitation · physical interaction (clicking, dragging, pointing) · early English oral production.
9 Learning Activities Listen & Touch: Children hear a word and tap the correct image. Repeat After Me: Children listen and repeat simple words. Drag & Match: Match images (no reading required). Trace & Play: Trace shapes or letters with visual guidance. Sing Along: Short songs to reinforce vocabulary.
10 Feedback Feedback is immediate, visual, and encouraging: sounds, animations, and stars reinforce participation. No penalties for mistakes. Teacher provides positive reinforcement and models correct pronunciation.
11 Interactivity The resource promotes active participation through play, allowing children to: touch, drag, and explore · listen and repeat · engage physically and verbally. All activities are designed to match short attention spans and developmental needs.
— Target Audience Young learners aged 2 to 5 years old, at a Pre-A1 emergent level, in early childhood education settings (nursery, preschool, kindergarten).
